## Overview
In this informative session, Sherman the CPA explains the essentials of sole proprietorships and the tax implications for those who operate as independent contractors, freelancers, or business owners. A sole proprietorship is defined as an unincorporated business owned by a single individual, and the tax process for sole proprietors differs significantly from that of W-2 employees due to the lack of tax withholdings.

The discussion covers how sole proprietors file their taxes, highlighting that the IRS treats the individual and their business as one entity. This means that the deadline for filing taxes aligns with personal tax deadlines. Sole proprietors report their business income using Schedule C, where they also include all ordinary and necessary business expenses to accurately calculate their taxable income and reduce their tax liability.

Sherman outlines the three main types of taxes that sole proprietors are responsible for: federal income taxes, state income taxes, and self-employment taxes. Additionally, he emphasizes the need for sole proprietors to make estimated tax payments if they expect to owe $1,000 or more in taxes, providing insights into the two primary methods for calculating these payments.

This session is a valuable resource for anyone looking to understand the tax responsibilities associated with sole proprietorships and offers tips on how to minimize tax obligations effectively.
